# Island flow for FINN: summary report

FINN accelerators turned into bitstreams by **parallel per-node synthesis, parallel
place-and-route of "islands" of nodes, RapidWright stitching and a short final assembly into a
pre-built shell**, instead of one global Vivado run. Branch `feature/rapidwright-islands`;
detailed log in `NOTES_ISLANDS.md`, raw timing data in `data/`, breakdown tables regenerated by
`summarize_breakdown.py`. Status: 2026-10-07 (evening: root-cause pass on the serial assembly,
see "What was wrong and what changed").

## Results at a glance

Wall time from the FINN frontend model (after FIFO sizing) to a bitstream, 100 MHz, each build
alone on a 64-core / 128-thread EPYC 9554P with 755 GB. All builds: 0 routing errors, setup and
hold met. "before" = the island flow before this round (timed 2026-10-06).

| board | model | baseline flow | baseline | island flow before | island flow now | speedup now |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ZCU104 | TFC | FINN Vivado ZynqBuild | 644 s | 369 s | 323 s | 1.99x |
| ZCU104 | CNV | FINN Vivado ZynqBuild | 891 s | 436 s | 393 s | 2.27x |
| ZCU104 | CNV PE=SIMD=1 | FINN Vivado ZynqBuild | 811 s | 425 s | 424 s | 1.91x |
| ZCU104 | VGG10 | FINN Vivado ZynqBuild | 2112 s | 1434 s | 1205 s | 1.75x |
| ZCU104 | MobileNet (ZCU104 folding) | FINN Vivado ZynqBuild | 5871 s | 5801 s | (not re-run) | ~1.0x |
| U55C | TFC | global Vivado, same shell | 1528 s | 738 s | 722 s | 2.12x |
| U55C | VGG10 | global Vivado, same shell | 2980 s | 1697 s | 1397 s | 2.13x |
| U55C | MobileNet (U250 folding, 398k LUT) | global Vivado, same shell | 5424 s | 2713 s | 1989 s | 2.73x |

Functional checks of the new builds (gate-level netlist of the stitched accelerator vs FINN's
stitched-IP RTL, xsim, `verify_accel.py`): ZCU104 TFC 16 amplitude-varied frames and VGG10 8
frames, identical outputs (varying classes). Fair comparisons use the same shell and the same
Vivado settings on both sides (FINN's Zynq strategies on the ZCU104, Vivado/v++ defaults with
phys_opt on the U55C). The island flow's shell is built once per board / clock / DMA interface
(~6 min ZCU104, ~20 min U55C) and cached; the baselines synthesize the shell IPs in every build.
Against the Vitis platform flow on the U55C (TFC / VGG10 / MobileNet 4860 / 7307 / 10756 s) the
ratios are larger, but not like for like (v++ re-implements the much larger Vitis platform per
model; the island flow's U55C shell is a small Vivado-only XDMA shell, not XRT-compatible).

## Why synthesis, placement and routing look alike in the native flow

From the baseline runs' own logs (ZCU104, seconds; synth_design in parentheses):

| | TFC | CNV | VGG10 | MobileNet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OOC synthesis of the accelerator | 72 (48) | 171 (142) | 421 (359) | 1066 + **3161** |
| OOC synthesis of each shell IP (in parallel; SmartConnect longest) | 49-128 | 49-128 | 50-129 | 50-130 |
| link_design | 21 | 25 | 81 | 82 |
| opt / place / phys_opt / route | 6 / 78 / 0 / 36 | 9 / 126 / 0 / 79 | 50 / 416 / 37 / 250 | 48 / 480 / 39 / 386 |
| post-placement WNS (10 ns clock) | +5.6 ns | +4.3 ns | +5.5 ns | +5.6 ns |
| reports + checkpoints inside impl_1 | 15 | 28 | 316 | 341 |

1. **100 MHz is a relaxed clock for UltraScale+.** Right after placement every model has
   4.3-5.8 ns of slack on a 10 ns period, so the placer and the router converge in one pass with
   no timing-driven iterations. "P&R dominates" is what happens at high utilization and tight
   clocks; here P&R is cheap.
2. **Small designs are mostly fixed cost.** A Vivado run costs ~45 s before useful work (start
   7 s, part load 9 s, synth_design of an 8-bit adder 16 s, write_checkpoint 7 s, exit ~7 s), and
   the block design's OOC synthesis runs ~10 IP runs of 50-130 s each in parallel. For TFC/CNV
   the synthesis stage is dominated by that overhead, while their P&R (5-25k LUT on a 230k-LUT
   device) is small.
3. **FINN's RTL is synthesis-heavy.** Weights and thresholds are constants (LUT ROMs, BRAM
   initialization), and Flow_PerfOptimized_high disables LUT combining and resource sharing and
   keeps equivalent registers. One MobileNet partition (a 1024-channel Thresholding_rtl in LUT
   ROM) spends 51 of its 53 minutes in synthesis's "Cross Boundary and Area Optimization".
4. **Not P&R either:** VGG10/MobileNet's impl_1 spends ~5 min writing reports and checkpoints.

## What was wrong and what changed (2026-10-07)

The largest serial step was the assembly's route_design (VGG10 ZCU104: 287 s, U55C MobileNet:
1080 s), about as long as the baseline's whole global route, although the accelerator arrives
routed. Opening the assembly in Vivado (via the Vivado MCP) showed it starting with 1124 failed
nets and 752 node overlaps (VGG10). Root causes and fixes:

| problem | evidence | fix |
|---|---|---|
| Zynq shell routing ran through the island region (CONTAIN_ROUTING does not hold PS8 nets, also with the PS8 in the pblock) | 434 of 12.4k shell nets, ~43k node uses in INT columns 30-34; islands collided with them | shell strip 5 columns + one empty moat column; `shell_escape.txt` reports leftovers (28 nets, next column only) |
| adjacent islands shared INT columns | URAM tiles connect to INT x and x+1; 5693 conflicting nets | device model merges each URAM column pair |
| tiny islands from the exact-k min-max cut | 16 islands of cost 66-900 in single columns; stitch could not reach them (252 s, 56 pins left) | merge islands < 15 % of the largest, >= 3 columns |
| U-shaped islands | MobileNet U55C island routed 15k overlaps for > 25 min | skyline only accepts monotone staircases (rectangle, L, stairs) |
| boundary pins boxed in by island routing | out-of-context port nets are unrouted (Vivado Route 35-198); assembly router could not get 4 IODMA nets out | `HD.PARTPIN_RANGE` on shell-boundary ports; stitcher rips up blocking island nets (RWRoute soft preserve) and stays in the island region |
| unregistered kernel reset across the device | U55C MobileNet assembly critical path, WNS -0.99 ns at router start | 2-FF reset pipeline per island |
| IODMA islands far from the shell | U55C MobileNet: input IODMA island in SLR2, 8.7 ns from the shell | Alveo: islands with shell ports anchored next to the shell, placed first |
| island hold after the clock is re-routed at assembly | TFC: 12 island-internal paths at -37..-4 ps | islands routed with 50 ps hold uncertainty |
| FINN prep ran IODMA HLS partition by partition | ~50 s each, serialized | all partitions' HLS concurrently (spawned processes) |

Not adopted, measured: routing only the remaining nets with the interactive router
(`route_design -nets`, opt-in `FINN_RWI_ASM_ROUTE=incremental`): 23 s on TFC, but its
initialization still places the clock (57 s on VGG10) and it gives up on nets it may not rip up
(VGG10: one net, after 170 s), so the full route_design stays the default; pre-routing the static
nets: no effect. The ZCU104 assembly route without conflicts is now ~150-260 s for VGG10, of which
~130 s is router initialization (RT build, ILP clock placement, timing update) that does not
depend on how much is left to route.

Refactor: `profiles.py` (the baseline's Vivado settings), `assembly.py` (assembly script),
`plan_islands` (island count / packing search, dry-runnable without Vivado), `island_region`,
`shell_anchor`, `merge_small`, `boundary_ports`; the stitcher reports unrouted nets and PIP
conflicts; island route-error check ignores partition-pin stubs.

## Critique: why not 10x, and what it would take

The island flow parallelizes what scales with the design (synthesis and P&R of the dataflow
layers) and keeps a serial tail. Its wall time is bounded below by its critical path:

    FINN prep (IODMA HLS)  +  synthesis of the largest node  +  P&R of the largest island
      +  stitching  +  assembly (open shell, read accelerator, route boundary, bitstream)

None of these terms shrinks with more cores. Measured floors on this machine:

* Every Vivado run costs ~45 s before useful work on a small design; an island P&R costs
  >= ~100 s on the xczu7ev and >= ~220 s on the xcu55c even when small; write_bitstream 20-40 s
  (ZCU104) / 55-120 s (U55C); reading the stitched accelerator 20-90 s / 90-280 s (Vivado parses
  RapidWright's EDIF and, inside read_checkpoint -cell, re-applies every shell constraint to the
  merged design: 77 s of MobileNet's read are one PCIe-IP power constraint with wildcard nets);
  route_design initialization ~130 s for VGG10, ~5 min for MobileNet on the U55C.
* A single dominant node bounds both parallel stages: VGG10's 37.7k-LUT / 384-DSP MVAU takes
  ~214 s to synthesize and ~410 s to place and route alone; the ZCU104 MobileNet's 1024-channel
  Thresholding_rtl takes 53 min to synthesize with the baseline's settings.

So small models (TFC, CNV) cannot go below ~250-300 s with Vivado in the loop: 2-3x is the
ceiling whatever the parallelism. Large models are bounded by the dominant node plus the tail:
MobileNet on the U55C now has ~270 s synthesis, ~440 s islands, ~130 s stitch and a ~1060 s
assembly tail (read 273, route 588, bitstream 121, reports 77): 2.7x; with the remaining tail
items fixed (constraint re-application, router initialization) perhaps 3-4x, not 10x.

An order of magnitude needs one of:

1. **No global step at all**: a pre-implemented static region with a fixed grid of
   reconfigurable partitions and fixed-width stream links between them (DFX overlay). Each
   island is implemented against an abstract shell and writes its own partial bitstream, in
   parallel; the serial tail disappears (configuration = static + partials). Costs: fixed link
   widths (FINN streams need width adaptation), partition-sized granularity, partial-bitstream
   deployment.
2. **Reuse across builds**: cache synthesized nodes and implemented islands by content hash.
   Iterations (folding, FIFO sizing) change a few nodes; the DynaRapid branch showed warm TFC
   builds of 128 s. Helps iteration loops, not cold builds.
3. **Split dominant nodes** (by PE / output channel) in FINN before synthesis, so no node bounds
   synthesis and island P&R; prefer node implementations that synthesize fast (thresholds in BRAM
   where the baseline allows it).

## Known problems and limits

* **Serial tail.** Even conflict-free, the assembly pays router initialization on the whole
  design (~130 s VGG10, ~5 min MobileNet U55C) plus reading the accelerator and the bitstream.
* **One huge node dominates synthesis and island P&R** (see the critique); the ZCU104 MobileNet
  (single island, 53-min node) gains nothing.
* **Dense designs.** VGG10 fills the ZCU104's DSPs (85 % of the island region), so it gets 4
  islands at 70 % LUTs; CNV PE=SIMD=1 needs the fabric above the PS (its nets across the shell
  are routed at assembly) and gains nothing from this round. The shell strip and moat cost the
  islands a BRAM and a DSP column on the xczu7ev.
* **Shell constraints.** read_checkpoint -cell re-applies the shell's constraints to the merged
  design; on the U55C an XDMA/PCIe power constraint with wildcard nets costs ~77 s per build.
* **QoR.** Islands at 50-80 % LUT utilization; all builds meet 100 MHz, higher clocks not studied.
* **Vitis platforms.** A cached shell inside the Vitis platform (nested DFX) failed on platform
  DFX rules; the U55C numbers use a Vivado-only shell that XRT cannot run.
* **Memory bursts.** ~80 Vivado runs and ~310 GB (sum of peaks) during node synthesis; island
  P&R ~120 GB; fewer parallel runs on smaller machines (Vivado slot limit).
* **Functional coverage.** Gate-level simulation against FINN's RTL (TFC, VGG10 this round;
  earlier CNV, MobileNet half-PE kernels); no FPGA runs yet.
